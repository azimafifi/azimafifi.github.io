Module Transformation

Converting a semester-long curriculum into a comprehensive year-long learning program. This study is being documented as the work happens.

Role
Curriculum designer & lecturer
Status
In progress, 2026
Publishing
[Expected month, 2026]

A semester gives you fourteen weeks to change how someone thinks. Most modules spend the first three re-teaching what the previous one already covered. This project stretches one module's arc across a full year so that learning compounds instead of restarting.

Why do this

Semester-long modules optimize for coverage: get through the syllabus, assess, reset. Retention suffers, and so does depth. A year-long program can sequence for spacing, revisiting, and application, the things learning science says actually make knowledge stick.

Name the specific module being transformed, the retention or performance problem observed in past cohorts, and the institutional constraints (accreditation, timetabling, assessment windows).

The planned structure

  1. Audit the current semester. Map every learning outcome, assessment, and week against what students demonstrably retain a semester later.
  2. Re-sequence across the year. Spread core concepts over spaced encounters: introduce, apply in studio, revisit through critique, assess in context.
  3. Redesign assessment as checkpoints. Smaller, more frequent evidence of learning replacing the single end-loaded submission.
  4. Pilot, measure, iterate. Run with one cohort, compare retention and outcome quality against the previous semester-only model.
